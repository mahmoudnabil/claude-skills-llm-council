# Mode: decide (research decisions)

For choices with real stakes: which venue, how to frame the paper, which
contribution set to claim, which experiments to add before submission,
whether to split one paper into two, whether to submit now or wait.

## Extra brief fields

- The options, stated as a list (A, B, C). If the user gave one idea,
  state "do X vs do not".
- Paper or project status: what results exist today, what is planned.
- Deadlines for each option, page limits, and acceptance rates when known
  (do not guess acceptance rates).
- Constraints: student graduation timelines, funding reporting, co-author
  commitments, prior rejections and their reviews.

## The five lenses

**Seat 1, Contrarian.** Finds the fatal flaw in each option. Venue scope
mismatch, a reviewer pool likely to be hostile, a framing the results do
not support, an experiment that will not finish in time or could weaken
the paper.

**Seat 2, First Principles.** Asks what the decision is for: citations,
a student's graduation, a grant report, a tenure dossier, a long-term
research line. Rebuilds the choice from that goal. May conclude the
question is the wrong one.

**Seat 3, Expansionist.** Looks for the upside: a bigger framing the
results already support, a stronger venue, a second paper hiding in the
work, a dataset or tool release, a follow-on proposal.

**Seat 4, Outsider.** Reads the abstract and contributions cold, as a
reviewer or program committee member from outside the subfield would.
Says what the paper appears to be about and whether that matches the
option being chosen.

**Seat 5, Executor.** Converts each option into a calendar: tasks, owners,
compute or data needs, and the date the submission would actually be
ready. Flags options that do not fit the deadline.

### Options Compared

### The Recommendation

[shared sections from SKILL.md: Clashes, Blind Spots, Action List, One Thing]
